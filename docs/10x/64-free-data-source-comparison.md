# Free data sources for 10X: research and comparison

Reviewed 21 October 2026, Australia/Sydney. Scope: Solana tokens in their first 24 hours, particularly Pump/PumpSwap; grading, manipulation review, launch valuation, holders, creator history and replayable evidence. This is public-documentation research, including Birdeye's rendered Free-plan controls and feature matrix. No authenticated API benchmark, account creation, subscription, funding, signing, integration or deployment occurred. Limits below are published offers, not measured coverage or granted product rights.

## Recommendation

Our batch-price foundation is a reasonable free choice. Our intelligence layer is incomplete. The next investigation should compare **Jupiter Tokens V2 organic metrics**, **GeckoTerminal/CoinGecko candles and GT Score**, and **GMGN's wallet/manipulation labels**, rather than making GMGN the assumed winner. **Birdeye Standard** is a useful small-cohort candle/holder alternative. **Helius Free** deserves a separate RPC/history assessment. Keep independent finalized transaction, mint and inventory validation.

These are technical priorities conditional on actual endpoint access and permitted use. In particular, Jupiter's linked licence restricts competitive analysis, sharing API content and combining it with outside data, while describing swap products; applicability to Tokens-only research needs clarification before a paired API experiment. Birdeye's general terms restrict capture/storage and third-party data sharing absent permission; supplemental API terms may change that. Free access alone does not clear our frozen evidence, derived public rankings or exports. See the rights section below.

## What we use today

The [collector documentation](../../discovery/README.md), [market adapter](../../discovery/src/dex-market.ts) and [Worker](../../discovery/src/worker.ts) show:

- DEX Screener is the configured default market provider, batching up to 30 mints and matching the exact retained canonical pool. GeckoTerminal remains selectable. This is a code/default review, not a fresh private-configuration or runtime-health audit.
- A central collector shares 600 market requests/day and 5,000 RPC calls/day; quality's 1,800 and wallets' 800 are subsets of RPC, not additional allowances. Market batches run every three minutes; source limits, pause, backoff, leases and body bounds remain.
- Finalized on-chain checks establish Pump creation, PumpSwap migration, mint authorities, supply/inventory and sampled funding/trades. Provider pool timestamps and wallet labels cannot replace those checks.
- Score-v3 approximates market integrity from retained quote samples and bounded trades. It lacks a complete OHLCV/trade tape, independently established organic wallets, exhaustive creator history and actual launch valuation for late-discovered tokens.

There is no evidence here that all providers below cover the same exact mint, launch interval or pool. A provider's latest quote cannot reconstruct the USDF chart's original opening cap. An apparent $5M opening needs contemporaneous price and supply evidence, and candle coverage beginning after graduation does not cover the bonding curve.

Buying power, high volume, green candles, many buyers, terminal verification and a favourable third-party score do not certify safety. Neither multiple vendors reading the same pool nor GeckoTerminal and CoinGecko independently validate a claim. Avoid double-counting related labels and shared upstream feeds.

## Free request economics

These are planning calculations, not permission to use every published allowance or a claim of measured responses:

- DEX Screener: refreshing 100 mints takes at most four token-batch requests before canonical-pool omissions/errors. Our lower 600/day collector ceiling still controls.
- Jupiter: up to 100 exact-mint lookups fit one documented search request. Hourly fetching would be 24 HTTP calls/day, but retaining every token observation can still exceed our research-record budget. Start with a small daily paired cohort, not full-list hourly research capture.
- Birdeye: the current CU table prices overview at 18, holder at 30, and a candle response with at most 300 items at 30. One such three-call investigation costs 78 CU/mint; ten mints/day for 30 days costs 23,400 CU before retries, larger pages, backfill or other use. Twenty/day would cost 46,800 and exceed Free. A 24-hour one-minute window has 1,440 possible bars and needs additional paging/appropriate higher-size pricing. [Endpoint CU costs](https://data.birdeye.so/docs/guides/what-is-compute-unit-cost.md).
- A CoinGecko Demo 10k-credit month cannot sustain the current collector's 600 calls every day (18k over 30 days). It can support a bounded candle/GT-score subset; the legacy public endpoint has a different access contract.
- DexPaprika's 100k rolling credits is not 100k multi-token HTTP requests: batch items and delivered stream updates are charged individually. It cannot supply Free one-minute candles regardless of unused credits.
- GMGN still needs weighted accounting, not only request counts; preserve the reviewed limits and example in milestone 63. Helius requires method-weight accounting. No automatic top-ups, paid fallback or multi-account quota evasion.

## Rights and data-quality preflight

Before any new adapter or paired API experiment, record the exact API product, endpoint/version, account tier and applicable API-specific licence. Resolve permitted internal comparison, mixed-source derived scoring, public display/attribution, retention for immutable replay, raw/derived export and redistribution. Public terminal visibility and MIT-licensed skills are not data licences.

- **Jupiter:** linked API agreement §2 describes internal integration but uses swap-specific definitions and labelling. §3.2(d), (f) and (g) restrict sharing API content, competitive analysis/benchmark disclosure, and combination with outside content. Ask whether separate Tokens V2 terms allow our described use. Do not infer that the Free marketing offer overrides this agreement, or label token metrics as swap output. [API agreement](https://developers.jup.ag/docs/legal/sdk-api-license-agreement).
- **Birdeye:** general terms §10.2 restrict capture/storage, sharing and competing uses; supplemental terms take precedence if applicable. Obtain the relevant Data API permission rather than assuming a developer key allows retained/re-exported evidence. [Terms](https://birdeye.so/data-api/terms-of-service).
- **DEX Screener:** API terms permit commercial use subject to a direct-competition restriction and no unauthorized API resale. Our product scope is not automatically cleared; retain the existing production-use review. [API terms](https://docs.dexscreener.com/api/api-terms-and-conditions).
- **CoinGecko:** current Demo pricing omits a commercial licence; paid commercial licences distinguish app integration from redistribution. Resolve the legacy GeckoTerminal contract separately, and do not assume a paid plan is needed or approved merely because Demo differs. [Plan/licence comparison](https://www.coingecko.com/en/api/pricing).
- Other providers: product-use, retained raw fixtures and exports remain unverified unless recorded explicitly. Request clarification only where public/API-specific terms do not answer it. Do not send support messages without the user's instruction.

For fixtures, retain requested and returned chain/mint/pool identities, provider/event/fetch times, finality where available, units, source schema and payload hash. Check seconds versus milliseconds, percent versus fraction, string booleans, absent versus zero, base versus quote orientation, candle intervals/empty intervals, cap versus FDV, response truncation and historical coverage. Solana addresses are case-sensitive. New fields must have provenance and cannot backdate frozen v1–v3 artifacts.

## Ordered continuation and acceptance criteria

This broadens milestone 63's G0–G4 plan; it does not activate additional score versions or collectors.

1. **G0: access, rights and endpoint contract.** First resolve Jupiter Tokens-only terms and legacy GeckoTerminal/CoinGecko access; then GMGN access/rights and Birdeye supplemental terms. Independently assess Helius Free method entitlements. Publish confirmed and unknown items; do not fund a GMGN account, pay Codex activation or start trials as dependencies.
2. **G1: small query-only fixtures.** Once permitted, collect exact-mint/pool responses for USDF, XTO and Kimchi as known diagnostic cases, plus ordinary and adversarial controls chosen without conditioning on vendor coverage. Validate identities, time windows, missingness and CU accounting before building disabled-by-default adapters. Known cases are development fixtures, not held-out validation.
3. **G2: prospective comparisons in stages.** First compare custom v3 with Jupiter activity metrics and a separately labelled GT-score/candle baseline. Compare GMGN's pinned due-diligence formula and full screening workflow as specified in milestone 63 when its access clears. Use Birdeye on a focused candle/holder subset to resolve useful disagreements. Do not build every vendor adapter or add every possible score arm at once.
4. **G3: decide on added information.** Measure exact new-token/launch/pool coverage, delay, missing fields, duplicate signals, quota cost and reproducibility; assess independent false positives and adverse outcomes on a later time split. Keep covered-only and full-inventory denominators. Avoid publicly publishing provider performance benchmarks where terms restrict them. A withheld price/low score is not itself a scam label or outcome ground truth.
5. **G4: versioned adoption only if justified.** Prefer validated indexed candles/metrics over duplicative collection approximations where they meet the same obligation. Preserve independent origin, authority, inventory, funding and launch-purchase evidence, adverse ceilings and historical replay. A favourable vendor score cannot cancel observed adverse evidence. Otherwise retain v3 and use the extra source only for investigation.

Every proposed collection call, retry and comparison record must reserve capacity before work. Use the existing global 600/day market and 5,000/day RPC ceilings; carve a proposed maximum 20 market HTTP calls/day for the first new-source fixture stage **inside** 600, with lower provider-specific monthly/weighted caps. Never silently add a new allowance per provider. Query transport needs an explicit fixed-host read-only allowlist; the existing relay does not already permit new hosts.

Research remains at the shared 100 records / 10 MB per day, three captures and five finalizations per maintenance pass, with all comparison/raw records counted. Inspect actual spare capacity and record sizes before selecting a cohort; do not promise ten daily pairs if existing research has used the allowance. A full day holds intake instead of overwriting evidence. Preserve pause/backoff, request/body/storage bounds, cache-only public reads and all election/action gates. Retention rights must support immutable research before capture; a deletion obligation cannot be hidden behind a hash.
