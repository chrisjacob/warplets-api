H1|1 Product direction and scope
H2|1.1 Purpose and terminology
10X identifies an existing project attracting attention and creates a new ATT token, such as ANSEM10X, paired with that project's original token, such as ANSEM. In this PRD, ATT denotes the new attention token and OG denotes the original project's paired token. These are operational definitions; the handoff does not require a particular expanded acronym. An ATT has its own price curve and is not a redemption claim on the OG project or a promise of a tenfold return.
The brand proposition is “We don't VAMP. We AMPlify.” The product should strengthen attention around existing communities. Curated daily launches are the acquisition event; permanent Genesis identity, seasonal collecting, staking and useful content support repeat participation. Solana is the initial chain. Future chains require demonstrated demand and a separate business case.

Vault|Community|Own Genesis or Season NFTs and claim their remaining ATT allocations.
Stake|Conviction|Stake ATT and earn the same ATT continuously during its ten day campaign.
Earn|Collective Action|Submit useful content and compete in one global daily Top 100.
ENDTABLE

The first useful product is a desktop and mobile website with curated markets, a wallet connection, transparent transaction states, and the financial modules that have actually passed their release gates. Exclude complex discovery, autonomous treasury agents, multichain accounting, bespoke NFT exchanges, bulk AI artwork generation and speculative integrations. Keep the Stonklets 1% liquidity / 1% holder / 1% operations arrangement separate from new ATT economics.
H2|1.3 Constraints and success
The founder is the initial product owner, curator and operator, with AI-assisted development and less than US$100 in operating runway. The architecture must minimize fixed expenses, irreversible deployment costs and manual work. Paid infrastructure requires a concrete cost justification. Randomized paid NFT features, financial claims and public incentive programs also need suitable legal review before their respective launches.
Success means genuine recurring participation and realized revenue that pays for operation. Token FDV, unclaimed reward valuations, founder inventory and nominal sellouts are not cash revenue. Release gates in section 17 are proposed operating thresholds, not forecasts or agreed commercial targets.

H1|3 User journeys and experience
H2|3.1 Primary journeys
The operator reviews a candidate's actual mint, pool and community evidence, prepares an immutable launch plan, checks the cost packet and allocation destinations, approves with the authorized signer, and watches confirmation and reconciliation. An unknown signature outcome remains pending until resolved; the operator cannot simply launch again. A failed gate leaves the candidate unpublished or explicitly skipped.
A trader opens a public ATT URL without signing in, sees the OG relationship, nominal and executable pricing, current launch fee and liquidity risk, connects a supported Solana wallet, obtains a fresh quote, reviews the exact transaction and signs. The interface distinguishes rejection, expiry, submission, confirmation and finality. It then offers Stake and relevant Earn content without forcing either action.
A staker selects an ATT, reviews campaign end time and the continuous reward estimate, deposits, observes accrued rewards, and can harvest or withdraw immediately. Claim-and-restake is offered only when the adapter can execute it safely; otherwise the two independent actions are explicit. A user can stake without a Farcaster identity.
A contributor stakes at least $10 of the relevant ATT, submits one URL for that token and day, sees eligibility evidence and moderation status, and receives a reasoned score. The daily board uses their best eligible contribution. The reward page shows every active token allocation, its unlock schedule, claimed amounts and any burn remainder. More stake alone never increases content points.
A Genesis collector checks whitelist status, tranche price, remaining inventory and rarity commitment, then pays and mints through a fair assignment process. The resulting NFT inherits its reserved historical entitlements under the approved vesting policy. The collector can claim vested amounts, refresh its image, or transfer the NFT with remaining claims attached.
A Season collector sees the season start, current Level, exact benefit expiry and historical Vault balance before buying. They may combine two eligible same-Level NFTs to upgrade one and reroll the other's Level, or pay to reroll visual traits. All quotes state costs, probabilities, affected NFTs and resulting expiry rules before signing.
H2|3.2 Information architecture
Use a small primary navigation: Markets, Vault, Stake, Earn and Collect. Markets contains lightweight Trending, advisory Votes and Launched views. A wallet menu exposes history, settings and notification consent. Operator controls are a separate authorized surface. NFT collection pages may use nft.10x.meme and season.10x.meme, but share contracts, components and identity with the main product.
Public routes should include /markets/{launchId}, /collections/genesis, /seasons/{seasonId}, /nfts/{assetId} and /earn/{epochId}. Preserve canonical URLs and render meaningful title, description, OG image and initial public content through targeted prerendering or server rendering. Private financial dashboards and drafts are not indexed. Host embedding must not create duplicate canonical pages.
H2|3.3 Frontend acceptance requirements
Use React, TypeScript, Vite, Tailwind v4, shadcn/ui, TanStack Router and TanStack Query as the preferred stack, subject to a tested version matrix. Create a small token set for spacing, typography, color, focus and status. Neutral surfaces and standard controls carry the application; brand artwork and selective accents carry personality. Financial values use clear units and consistent decimal formatting.
Review representative screens at 360, 390, 768 and 1440 CSS pixels. Desktop charts and tables must remain useful, with mobile summaries and explicit access to underlying values. Keyboard operation, visible focus, labelled inputs, appropriate contrast, reduced-motion support and accessible transaction announcements are required. Target WCAG 2.2 AA for core flows, with documented exceptions resolved before public launch.
Required reference screens are market detail and quote, combined Vault, Stake position, submission and leaderboard, Genesis mint, Season upgrade and transaction recovery. Capture desktop/mobile regression images after design approval. Real mobile wallet return/deep-link behavior requires physical-device verification; a browser emulator alone is insufficient.
H1|4 ATT launch and market requirements
H2|4.1 Launch policy
LAUNCH-01 Confirmed. The founder selects at most one suitable project per Sydney launch day. Votes advise selection and do not earn rewards. Publish the intended local window using Australia/Sydney so daylight saving is handled correctly. The founder expects launches within roughly a 60-minute range and will usually skip a missed window. Around 6 am and around 9 pm are alternatives; neither has been selected. An automatic timer is unnecessary for this design.
Record planned window, actual on-chain market activation T and any skip reason separately. Fees, Vault vesting, Stake emissions and this token’s Earn release windows use T. A skip creates no campaign, budget or placeholder token. Proposed capacity guard: if an early launch would overlap an eleventh active campaign, wait for the oldest campaign to finish or skip. Do not truncate an existing campaign.
LAUNCH-02 Confirmed. Prefer Meteora DAMM v2 with ATT as token A and OG as token B, single-sided ATT liquidity and no meaningful founder-funded OG reserve. The existing protocol laboratory required one raw unit of OG to initialize its tested path; accept that technical seed if validated for the selected mint. Verify mint decimals, transfer behavior, extensions, token program, pool bounds, route support and credible USD pricing before listing any OG. Unsupported token behavior blocks the launch rather than being silently normalized.
LAUNCH-03 Confirmed. Target nominal opening FDV around US$10000, subject to executable pool behavior. One billion total tokens is a candidate convention, not a fixed requirement. Store original supply S in integer base units, decimals, price bounds, token authorities, initial liquidity and all allocation amounts in the signed launch manifest. Validate every selected parameter against the deployed program, not only an SDK helper.
TABLE|Allocation|Share of original supply|Custody and timing
Liquidity|80%|Verified ATT/OG pool position; locking and fee rights explicitly tested.
Earn|10%|Ten budgets of 1% each; global daily winners, capped redistribution and daily unearned burns.
Stake|5%|Continuous same-token farm over ten days.
Genesis Vault|2.5%|All 10000 reserved NFTs share by immutable Level weight.
Season Vault|2.5%|All active Season NFTs and reserved active slots share at launch snapshot.
ENDTABLE
LAUNCH-04 Confirmed. If no Season allocation is active at launch, allocate the entire 5% Vault portion to Genesis. Freeze this decision with the launch snapshot; later Season activity cannot revise it. No founder initial token allocation or presale. Founder revenue is a separate fee/revenue flow. Select a supply divisible into these budgets in base units where feasible; otherwise publish a deterministic allocation rounding policy before signing. Never silently remove tokens from a promised budget to pay costs.
H2|4.2 Fee schedule and quotes
LAUNCH-05 Confirmed. Measure elapsed whole seconds from actual activation. At second 0 the fee is 90%; subtract one percentage point each second until the 1% floor. Second 88 is 2%, second 89 is 1%, and second 90 and later remain 1%. The 89-point reduction is absolute. Verify this exact schedule against executable pool quotes before funding.

The illustration divides nominal FDV by one minus the input fee and excludes price impact. The UI must instead prioritize the executable calculation: original token supply × OG actually spent × OG USD price / ATT actually received, with separate network costs. A quote shows timestamp, direction, actual total fee, minimum received, price impact, slippage tolerance and expiry. It must not present the nominal opening FDV as the buyer's executable cost.
Meteora documents a base fee plus optional dynamic component, a pool fee cap, and a default protocol deduction of 20% of the trading fee. OnlyB makes the OG the fee token, but buy and sell fee placement differs. Prefer dynamic fees disabled so the ongoing user fee remains 1% total; verify live configuration, cap and integer rounding. Under the documented default, company LP fee revenue is at most 80% of collected trading fees before other costs, assuming it owns all relevant fee rights. A 1% trading fee is therefore not 1% company revenue. [S1]
LAUNCH-06 Validation gate. Re-run captured-program and current deployed-program parity checks for the exact 80/10/5/2.5/2.5 funding, 90-second policy, single-sided initialization, permanent LP lock and fee collection rights. Test activation boundaries, both swap directions and any pre-activation paths. A high opening fee is not a guarantee against bots, MEV or manipulation. The old local proof establishes neither these new parameters nor production readiness. [R3, S2]
H2|4.3 Campaign lifecycle
Proposed states are draft, validated, approved, prepared, submitted, confirming, live, campaign ended and reconciled, with explicit failed, expired and unknown outcomes. Separate market life from campaign life: staking emissions and daily Earn allocations stop after ten days, but trading and vested claims can continue. Store the actual on-chain activation time; do not backdate a delayed launch to a planned time.
Revoke unnecessary mint and freeze authorities after verifying full allocation funding, and publish any authority intentionally retained. The exact order must avoid an unfunded but irreversible launch. The launch manifest includes hashes of artifacts and snapshots, recipient accounts, signer set, program identities, fees, maximum spend and rollback/recovery limits. No third-party route may replace instructions after approval without a fresh review and signature.
H1|5 Vault requirements
H2|5.1 Entitlements and timing
VAULT-01 Confirmed. A Vault is an entitlement associated with an NFT, not necessarily a separate token account per NFT. The current on-chain NFT owner controls remaining claims. Ownership transfers carry unclaimed allocations; amounts already claimed do not regenerate. Farcaster identity, a cached database owner or an old signed session cannot authorize a claim after ownership changes.
VAULT-02 Confirmed. For launch allocation A with start time T, vested(t) is zero through T + 24 hours, then A × (t − T − 24 hours) / 24 hours, capped at A at T + 48 hours. Use integer rounding down and subtract cumulative claimed. There is no catch-up unlock at the cliff. Tokens allocated but not yet claimed remain liabilities, not company inventory.
VAULT-03 Confirmed. Genesis uses the fixed total Level denominator 20130 from program inception, including every unminted slot in all ten tranches. For NFT Level L, launch allocation = Genesis budget × L / 20130. Multiple NFTs add allocations. Selling one NFT transfers only its remaining entitlement. Keep full-precision rational accounting or a proven integer remainder scheme; independently rounding every daily claim must not destroy entitlement over time or allow repeated dust claims.
VAULT-04 Confirmed. Seasons share their collective budget across all active Season NFTs and reserved active slots, using their Level at the launch snapshot. A subsequent upgrade, reroll, transfer or expiry never rewrites a past launch denominator. Expired NFTs receive no new allocation but retain past entitlements permanently. If total active Season weight is zero, the Genesis fallback applies.
H2|5.2 Unminted accrual and fair assignment
VAULT-05 Confirmed. Unminted Genesis and Season inventory accrues its full promised entitlement. Early minters must not divide the reserve belonging to later minters. Each reserve must be economically backed and reconcilable before those tokens are made available elsewhere. Neither the founder nor a buyer may cherry-pick a known rare NFT with a larger historical balance.
Proposed representation: reserve Genesis balances by immutable Level class and tranche, with a committed finite rarity inventory. A verifiable random allocation without replacement binds a purchased mint to its Level and stable NFT identifier. This can avoid ten thousand writes for every launch. It must be proven equivalent to full slot accrual, prevent operator reordering and selective aborts, and preserve exact remaining class quotas. If that proof fails, use committed slot records instead; cheaper representation cannot change allocation rights.
A published deterministic permutation whose future assignments are visible is insufficient: a buyer or operator could time purchases to known rare slots. Payment commitment, entropy source, reveal, retry and timeout rules must be designed together. Failed fulfillment retains the same draw or follows a disclosed refund rule, never a free redraw. Initial Season draws also use a fixed inventory without replacement; later upgrade-material rerolls use the original probability distribution independently.
VAULT-06 Delegated default. Minting binds the reserved entitlement to the NFT and preserves the original launch clock. At hour 36 a newly minted NFT can claim half its allocation; after hour 48 it can claim the full unclaimed amount immediately. A transfer changes the claimant only. This accepts the possibility that a buyer mints to claim mature balances; disclose that value and uncertainty in the mint quote. A second lock for historical balances is optional only if a later cost and security review justifies a policy change for future sales.
H2|5.3 Custody and claim implementation
Prefer pooled token custody per ATT and a claim-on-demand system. Separate allocation authority, token custody and read-only indexing. A proof must bind collection, NFT identifier or reserved class assignment, launch, amount, vesting and cumulative claim state; the on-chain claim path must verify current ownership and prevent replay after transfer. A wallet-address Merkle distributor by itself does not deliver transferable NFT entitlements.
Validation gate: select a reviewed contract/program that supports these semantics and affordable state. Existing Earn distributor work cannot be relabelled as a permanent NFT Vault. If a custom program is necessary, its review and deployment cost may exceed present runway. In that case keep this phase in simulation or seek funding; do not launch an insecure custody substitute or promise unsupported financial rights.
Show combined balances, per-NFT and per-ATT allocations, vested and claimed amounts, collection weights and history. Give indicative values with the price timestamp and liquidity caveats. Explain that an owner can claim before a sale settles unless the marketplace enforces a minimum remaining balance. Product policy permanently excludes a Burn NFT action for Genesis and Season NFTs across the web, optional Mini Apps and public API. Assess external burn behavior in the ownership and custody tests before choosing the NFT standard.
Permanent entitlement also requires durable claim data. Publish versioned, content-addressed manifests and proofs with independent backup/export so a website outage or company shutdown does not erase claimability. No automatic 365-day clawback may apply to allocated permanent Vault balances.
H1|6 Stake requirements
STAKE-01 Confirmed. Use Kamino Farms as the preferred candidate, not Kamino lending. Each ATT has a same-token farm funded with 5% of original supply for ten days. Rewards accrue continuously while staked; there is no daily cliff, warmup, withdrawal cooldown or mandatory lock. Users can harvest without withdrawing, withdraw at any time, and restake immediately. Claim-and-restake is a convenience only where supported safely.
STAKE-02 Confirmed. Fund Stake with exactly 5% of original supply. 10X targets no additional platform fee. Test whether the candidate takes a third-party charge and identify its payer, currency and timing. A measured deduction is a decision about what stakers receive; it does not authorize changing the supply split.
Example: for 1,000,000 ATT, 50,000 ATT fund the farm. If all rewards are earned and the farm hypothetically keeps 1% of each harvest, stakers collectively receive 49,500 ATT and the farm treasury receives 500 ATT. This is an illustration, not a reported Kamino rate. If a deduction is measured, show the exact take-home amount and alternative before selecting the farm. Do not silently top up rewards from another allocation.
For an ideal constant-emission farm, funded reward rate = 0.05 × S / 864000 seconds. A staker's instantaneous share is their staked units divided by total active staked units. Accumulate using the program's actual fixed-point accounting, settle before balance changes, and never use browser time to determine an authoritative reward. Rounding and empty intervals must conserve funded supply.
STAKE-03 Confirmed. Empty-farm intervals do not accrue retroactive windfalls for the next depositor. At campaign end, recover or burn only demonstrably unused emissions, preserving all earned but unclaimed liabilities and principal. The exact recovery destination and transaction are open. Ending emissions never disables principal withdrawals.
The inspected Kamino source exposes deposit warmup and withdrawal cooldown settings, and a global treasury fee that participates in harvest splitting. Its empty-stake path updates issuance time. These are encouraging implementation signals, not proof that a particular deployed farm has the required settings or zero charges. Verify same mint for deposit/reward, arbitrary ATT support, schedule rounding, actual global fee, admin powers, rent, recovery and deployed-source parity. [S3]
The repository licence inspected is Business Source License 1.1 with no additional use grant and a stated future change date. Invoking a deployed program and copying or operating a fork are different questions; confirm permitted SDK/program usage. Do not assume public source means unrestricted production reuse. Quarry is a fallback candidate if Kamino fails a requirement; it has not been validated here. Custom staking is the last option, subject to a new cost and security case. [S4]
H1|7 Earn requirements
H2|7.1 Submission and eligibility
EARN-01 Confirmed. Maintain one global daily Top 100, not a board per token. Allow one submitted content URL per active ATT per user per day, up to ten active campaigns. Score the user's highest eligible submission; if it fails eligibility, consider the next highest. One excellent contribution can compete with a user who submits for every campaign. Votes are not content rewards.
EARN-02 Confirmed. The user must have at least US$10 of the corresponding ATT staked both at submission and at the daily snapshot. Use the current main-pool market price and a validated OG-to-USD conversion. There is no price-drop grace period. An ATT qualifying one submission does not qualify content for a different ATT. More than $10 does not directly increase content points.
Proposed pricing policy: record pool state, source slot/time, OG quote, decimals and stake evidence for each check. Reject or mark pending if price evidence is missing, stale, outside approved bounds or under investigation; never count a cached last price as current without a bound. Use a manipulation detector to hold suspicious settlement for review, not silently substitute a different price formula. Final price freshness and liquidity thresholds require the feasibility spike.
EARN-03 Confirmed. Human moderation initially assigns accepted content an integer score 1–10; rejected content scores zero. Evaluate originality, relevance, accuracy where relevant, narrative understanding, creativity/humor and genuine contribution or distribution. Memes and cultural insight are valid alongside research. Preserve URL, content evidence/hash, submission time, moderator, score reason and revisions. Confirm creator control of the publishing identity where practical; a URL alone is not proof of authorship.
H2|7.2 Points and multiplier
TABLE|Component|Rule|Maximum
Onboarding|0.1X for each confirmed eligible action|1X
Content streak|0.1X for each qualifying day up to ten|1X
NFT Level|0.3X × highest eligible Level across Genesis and active Seasons|3X
Purchased stake participation|0.1X per qualifying token-day, at most fifty|5X
ENDTABLE
EARN-04 Confirmed. Multiplier = max(0.1, min(10, sum of components)). There is no automatic 1X base. NFT bonuses do not stack across assets. Earn points = content score × multiplier, giving 0.1–100 points. Store fixed-point integers, not binary floating point. One token-day is one qualifying token position per day, never each additional $10 or repeated deposits.
Onboarding — confirmed framework. The founder can enable, disable and add named actions without a frontend release. More than ten distinct actions can be offered over time. Each valid active completion contributes 0.1X, capped at 1X. Default: publish changes against a future board, preserve completion evidence and never recalculate a finalized board. A disabled action stops contributing for future boards; re-enabling it cannot produce a duplicate credit. Show users the effective date and the actions that currently count.
Store a stable action ID, eligibility rule, evidence verifier, effective epochs and one completion per user/action. Require a verifiable success signal; a click on a link is not proof of following. “Follow 10X on Farcaster” is a candidate, not a requirement to join via Farcaster. Other candidates include completing the launch-fee walkthrough or a Vault-claim tutorial with a knowledge check. The initial list can be selected later.
Content streak — confirmed rule. A rolling 24 hours with no approved, stake-eligible submission scoring at least 1 resets the streak. Missing content, only zero scores or no eligible stake cannot sustain it. Ranking outside the Top 100 does not by itself break a streak. Record the approval timestamp and both stake checks. At cutoff C count approvals in (C − 24h, C]; an unresolved check holds settlement, and an approval after C belongs to the next window. Increment at most once per global board, up to ten days. Detect a gap of 24 hours or more between qualifying approvals even if both adjacent boards contain a post; the next qualifying day restarts at one.
EARN-05 Delegated default. For the pilot, credit a purchase only when a supported swap and deposit into the linked wallet’s ATT farm position can be verified together in one confirmed transaction. Credit the lesser of net ATT bought and ATT deposited. An unrelated deposit, transfer, harvest or reward restake creates no credit. A user can still submit eligible content with ordinary staked ATT. If no route supports the combined transaction within the cost ceiling, return a measured alternative before enabling the purchase bonus.
Maintain one credited-unit balance per enrolled wallet and ATT. A withdrawal removes credited units first, down to zero; a redeposit does not restore them. An exit, transfer of the position or change of linked wallet does not carry credits to a new position. A fresh verified purchase and deposit may qualify, even if the buyer previously sold a reward: the rule verifies an actual purchase, not the source of the buyer’s funds.
At each global cutoff, record at most one token-day if the lesser of credited and actually staked units is worth at least US$10 at the snapshot price. Keep ten daily flags per position. Count flags from the last ten closed boards only for positions still meeting that threshold in an active campaign; clear flags on a complete exit, and expire them at campaign end. Sum across tokens, cap at fifty, and apply 0.1X per token-day. Example: five qualifying active positions on each of three boards yield fifteen token-days, or 1.5X. A price dip below threshold earns no flag and suspends that position’s bonus while it is below threshold.
Index only enrolled positions and supported transactions from enrollment. Deduplicate by signature and instruction; replay verified deposits and withdrawals after the last confirmed checkpoint, including direct farm withdrawals outside the app. Reconcile against the farm before scoring. If evidence is incomplete, hold that component pending reconciliation; never restrict principal withdrawals. Bound pilot enrollment by measured read, storage and moderation costs. Publish these limited routes and the treatment of withdrawals before users buy.
H2|7.3 Daily settlement and distribution
EARN-06 Confirmed. Select the top 100 users by points and publish a deterministic tie-break. Proposed ordering is points descending, content score descending, accepted submission timestamp ascending, then stable submission identifier. Freeze a user's reward wallet for the epoch before settlement. Reject duplicate wallet awards; a wallet cannot bypass its cap by linking to several user records. Separate account-linking policy from Sybil detection.
Each active ATT contributes 1% of its original supply for each of ten allocations. Every global winner receives a share of every active ATT's daily budget, regardless of which ATT their selected content covers. The per-wallet cap is 10% of each token's daily budget. These rules apply independently to each token, not to the USD value of the basket.
EARN-07 Confirmed. Allocate proportionally to points, freeze winners exceeding the 10% cap at that cap, and redistribute the remainder proportionally among uncapped winners. Repeat until no cap is exceeded or no eligible recipient remains. Floor final allocations in base units and burn the unallocated remainder, including rounding dust. Do not roll unearned rewards into another day. Record winner totals plus burn so they exactly equal the daily budget.
TABLE|Eligible winners and points|Result for one daily token budget B
0 winners|Burn B after a valid empty settlement; missing data is not a valid empty board.
5 equal winners|Each receives 10% of B; burn 50%.
10 winners with any positive points|Each receives 10% of B after capped redistribution; no unallocated amount except integer dust.
20 winners with one at 100 points and nineteen at 1|Top receives 10%; others share 90%, approximately 4.7368% each.
100 equal winners|Each receives 1% of B.
ENDTABLE
H2|7.4 Vesting and epoch mapping
EARN-08 Confirmed. A new ATT's first allocation uses the previous day's leaderboard and has a 24-hour cliff followed by 24-hour linear vesting. The handoff proposes non-overlapping subsequent windows. Recommended mapping: allocation d, for d = 1 through 10, uses the board for epoch e0 + d − 2; it vests from activation + d days to activation + (d + 1) days. The first window is hours 24–48 and the tenth is hours 240–264. Final vesting ends on day eleven. Earn winners can claim any remaining vested award indefinitely, including after campaign closure. Keep funded balances, proofs and claim records available until paid in full. Exclude every allocated amount from unused-budget burns and expiry sweeps.
Proposed global board rule: one UTC calendar day, midnight inclusive to next midnight exclusive, for everyone. Assign an event at midnight to the new submission epoch. For approval lookback, C is the final instant of the closing epoch at the stored timestamp precision. At actual launch T, set e0 to the UTC date containing T. Allocation 1 uses the immediately preceding UTC date, whether or not its results have been published. Allocation 2 uses e0; subsequent allocations advance one board at a time. No separate board is created for an early or late token. The UTC cutoff and subsequent vesting windows are engineering proposals; the actual launch clock is confirmed.
Example: an ATT goes live Tuesday at 10:37 UTC. Its first budget uses Monday’s board. Under the proposed schedule it starts vesting Wednesday at 10:37 and finishes Thursday at 10:37. A launch at 11:12 would move those release times by 35 minutes but would use the same board. If Monday’s moderation or pricing evidence is incomplete, keep its allocation pending; never substitute Sunday, burn it as empty or restart the vesting clock when evidence arrives.
First launch — confirmed. If no prior eligible campaign existed, finalize the first board as legitimately empty and burn the first ATT’s first daily Earn budget: 1% of original supply, not the entire ten-day 10% pool. Announce this before launch and record the burn once. A missing data feed or outstanding moderation is not evidence of an empty board.
H2|7.5 Distributor and moderation gates
Evaluate a Merkle/vesting distributor with exact 24-hour cliff plus 24-hour linear release, partial claims, replay protection and affordable state. Solana Foundation Rewards documents linear and cliff variants, but the inspected deployment table is empty and its audit covers a specified commit, not necessarily current main. A cliff-linear schedule that begins vesting before the cliff would release a catch-up amount and fail this PRD. Verify deployed binary, audited delta, start/cliff parameters, root authority, revocation and closure behavior. [S5]
The old Jito policy's one-second release and 365-day clawback are superseded. An adapted distributor is only a candidate until its policy, source and deployed behavior pass tests. Allocated rewards and unearned burns must remain separate. An application pause cannot stop direct claims on a program that has no equivalent pause capability. [R4]
Human capacity is a launch constraint. At 1000 contributors and ten submissions each, 10000 reviews at three minutes each would require 500 hours per day. Proposed pilot admission is bounded by an observed moderation budget, with clear campaign capacity before accepting work. Do not accept submissions that cannot be reviewed by the promised deadline. AI may later triage duplication or support scoring, but human moderators make the final scoring and eligibility decisions. Publish those decisions as final and record the evidence and any conflicts of interest. Proceed directly to settlement after all required checks pass. There is no contributor appeal period or re-scoring after publication.

H1|9 Season collection requirements
H2|9.1 Creation and benefit periods
SEASON-01 Confirmed. Create up to 1000 NFTs per monthly Season. Working paid mint price is 0.1 SOL, adjustable before the relevant phase. Each Season starts with the same Level distribution as a Genesis tranche and initial weight 2013. Attributes are Level, Model, Backdrop and Symbol. SEASON-02 Confirmed. Benefit expiry equals published Season start plus the NFT's current Level in months. It is not measured from mint or upgrade time. Add the Level in UTC calendar months to the Season start, using the last day of the target month when necessary; for example, January 31 plus one month becomes the last day of February at the same UTC time. An upgrade can reactivate an expired NFT only if the new Level moves expiry into the future. No NFT remains active more than ten months from Season start. Late mint pages must prominently show remaining benefit time; an NFT can inherit historical balances despite having little or no future eligibility.

H1|10 NFT standards and marketplace integration
Prefer Metaplex Core if ownership verification, mutable visual metadata, protected economic attributes, royalty controls and target marketplaces pass an end-to-end test. Metaplex currently documents a 0.0015 SOL protocol charge per Core asset mint, separate from rent and transaction costs. This makes lazy minting important: 1000 mints incur 1.5 SOL in that fee alone. Obtain a fresh exact quote before any purchase or backstop commitment. [S6]
Bubblegum v2 is a cost-research option, not the default. Its current documentation lists transfer/trading gaps for named wallets and marketplaces and requires DAS indexing. Validate current ecosystem support rather than assuming compressed NFTs can be sold like Core assets. Include tree initialization, proof retrieval and provider costs. Do not invent a bespoke Token-2022 NFT royalty system merely to reduce a small creation fee. [S7]
Implement a marketplace adapter for listings, bids/offers, buy, cancel, sale notifications and share links. Evaluate OpenSea, Magic Eden and Tensor against the selected Solana standard and actual executable transactions; a data API or logo in a support matrix is insufficient. MVP may link to a verified external execution surface if native trading integration fails cost or safety gates. Never represent an unsupported venue as integrated.
Core royalty configuration can be advisory or constrain transfer programs depending on ruleset. A restrictive ruleset may affect compatibility. Validate transfer, list, fill, cancel, private transfer and claim ownership on each supported venue. Genesis targets 5% royalties and Seasons 2%; expose actual enforcement limits. [S8]
The marketplace display must show current owner, Level, active/expired status, remaining Vault balances, price age and metadata state. Financial attributes and claim proofs use canonical on-chain identifiers rather than an image URL or marketplace cache. Reserve custody and mint proceeds never pass through an unverified marketplace adapter.
H1|11 API first architecture
H2|11.1 System boundaries
Use one modular backend on Cloudflare Workers with Hono, Zod validation and generated OpenAPI, backed by D1 for relational state and R2 for immutable manifests, evidence and media. Keep frontend, backend and shared domain contracts separated in source and builds. This is a modular monolith; it does not require nine services, nine databases or additional paid platforms.
The flow is web or optional host adapter → versioned API → domain services → persistence and protocol adapters → verified on-chain programs. The frontend displays server-produced decisions and prepares user interactions. It does not decide allocations, authority, launch eligibility, treasury policy or claimable amounts. On-chain custody programs enforce financial invariants even if a client bypasses the website.

Stake|Farm configuration and position projection|Earn purchase provenance policy

H2|11.2 Shared contracts and adapters
Proposed source layout is apps/web, apps/api, packages/contracts, packages/domain and packages/adapters, with existing protocol and QA laboratories retained separately. Exact paths can follow repository conventions. Financial domain functions should be deterministic, use integer/fixed-point values, and accept explicit clock, price evidence and policy version. One published policy version generates API descriptions, snapshots and test fixtures.
Protocol adapters expose prepare, simulate, verify, submit when authorized, and reconcile operations; domain services do not construct unchecked arbitrary instructions. Wallet Standard and Solana Kit are preferred, but exact versions and browser/Worker compatibility need validation. Avoid pulling experimental Anchor/native dependencies into the browser or Worker build. Maintain a dependency inventory and investigate known advisories rather than accepting a passing bundle alone.
Optional Farcaster/Base host adapters expose capabilities such as identity hint, wallet availability, sharing and notification permission. Feature-detect at runtime. A host hint is not authenticated wallet ownership. Preserve ordinary navigation, direct URLs and wallet sign-in outside the host; no duplicate embedded app or mandatory host component library.
H2|11.3 Read models and workload
Use indexed D1 queries and bounded pagination. Aggregate balances and campaign status into small read models; store large immutable manifests in R2. Do not create 10000 NFT-by-launch rows or accounts every day merely to show Genesis totals. Fixed Level weights support compact cumulative accounting, while Season changes require immutable launch snapshots and a tested ownership/Level history.
Proposed public market refresh is 15 seconds with backoff to 30/60/120 seconds on errors and pause while hidden. Cache immutable metadata for long periods and public summaries briefly; use explicit freshness indicators. Private balances, quotes and transaction intents are not shared-cache entries. Maintain separate cache keys by chain, policy, snapshot and visibility. Existing no-store/noindex local defaults must be deliberately revised for public discovery.
The free Worker CPU allowance is a constraint for signature verification, proof generation and snapshot calculation. Benchmark representative calls. Bound each job to checkpoints that resume under a fenced lease; precompute expensive manifests outside latency-sensitive routes. Prefer the existing job/outbox patterns before adding queues or another service, but introduce paid scheduling only if measurements justify it.
H1|12 API contract specification
H2|12.1 Conventions
Proposed base path is /api/v1 with a published OpenAPI document and schema-derived TypeScript client. Use stable opaque IDs for users, launches, epochs, intents and operations; include chain plus canonical mint/asset address where relevant. Represent token base units as decimal strings, prices as fixed-point decimal strings with explicit units, timestamps as UTC RFC 3339, and percentages as documented integer basis points or numerators. Do not transmit financial values as unsafe JavaScript integers.
List responses use items, nextCursor and snapshotId with a default page size 25 and proposed maximum 100. Filters and ordering are allowlisted; cursor pagination must remain stable when new items arrive. Single responses include data and meta containing requestId, policyVersion, asOf and sourceSlot when applicable. Cache headers and ETags are part of the contract for public resources.
Errors use code, message, retryable, requestId and optional fieldErrors/retryAfter. Distinguish UNAUTHORIZED, FORBIDDEN, VALIDATION_FAILED, ELIGIBILITY_FAILED, STALE_PRICE, QUOTE_EXPIRED, STATE_CONFLICT, IDEMPOTENCY_CONFLICT, DEPENDENCY_UNAVAILABLE and TRANSACTION_OUTCOME_UNKNOWN. HTTP status and retryability must agree; a 202 pending transaction is not a success receipt.

H2|12.3 Authentication and idempotency
Use opaque, revocable HttpOnly browser sessions with secure cookie and CSRF/origin controls, or carefully scoped bearer tokens for non-browser clients. Wallet challenges include a fresh nonce, domain, chain, issue/expiry times and intended action. Linked-wallet changes require proof of both relevant authorities and a published reward-wallet cutover policy. Privileged operator access adds a separate strong authentication boundary and signed financial approval.
Every mutating financial intent requires an Idempotency-Key scoped to principal, route and canonical request hash. Repeating the same request returns the same operation and state; changing the body under the same key returns conflict. Persist financial uniqueness permanently through intent/on-chain nonces even if an API response cache expires. Database uniqueness and compare-and-swap state transitions protect simultaneous requests.
Proposed initial rate limits are 60 public reads per minute per IP, 10 quote/preparation requests per minute per wallet, and a stricter challenge limit with backoff. Tune against measured traffic and shared-network fairness. Return Retry-After, impose payload/URL bounds and isolate expensive methods. Agent access initially supports discovery, research, monitoring and preparation with explicit scopes; it cannot access treasury keys, alter reward roots or autonomously deploy. An MCP server is deferred.
H2|12.4 Transaction state contract
An intent moves from created through validated, simulated and awaiting signature to submitted, confirmed and finalized. Rejected, expired and failed are explicit terminal outcomes; unknown is a reconciliable state, not permission to repeat the operation. Record attempts separately from the economic intent. A new blockhash or quote needs a new verified packet linked to the same still-unfulfilled intent.
The response contains operationId, action, chain/network, fee payer, required signers, packet digest, expected asset movements, maximum fees, minimum received, source snapshot, expiresAt/lastValidBlockHeight, simulation result and current status. Confirmed is provisional for accounting; finalization policy determines irreversible receipts. Verify packet contents and signer authority again after the client signs, and verify any externally supplied signature actually executes the intended action before settling records.

H1|14 Security and operating controls
H2|14.1 Financial authority
Separate low-balance operational fee payer, launch/deployer authority, company treasury and user reward custody. Keep private keys out of the browser, general API environment, logs and agent tools. Prefer hardware-backed human approval for privileged financial actions; use a reviewed multisignature arrangement when a genuinely independent second signer exists. A solo operator with two keys is not independent oversight.
Every privileged action has a role, spend ceiling, reason, evidence hash and audit event. Moderator access cannot release tokens; notification credentials cannot write allocations; image generation cannot change economic NFT attributes. Parameter changes apply prospectively under a new policy version. Explicitly record retained program upgrade/admin authority and its implications for users.
H2|14.2 Threat controls
TABLE|Threat|Required control and residual risk
Malicious transaction substitution|Verify full message, programs, account privileges, signers, amounts and lookup tables; reject unknown instructions and networks.
Replay or double claim|On-chain consumed/cumulative state plus unique intents; transfer does not reset a claim counter.
Price manipulation|Exact pool registration, fresh evidence, liquidity/manipulation checks and settlement hold; spot-price eligibility remains economically attackable.
Sybil reward farming|Wallet uniqueness, acquisition evidence, review signals and published sanctions; no claim of one-human enforcement.
Randomness manipulation|Committed operation, verifiable entropy, no selective redraw, bounded timeout and audited fulfillment.

Unsafe content URLs|Allowlisted protocols, private-network/redirect blocking, size/time limits and isolated retrieval; never execute submitted content.
Metadata or media abuse|Upload bounds, content moderation, provenance, safe serving headers and economic field separation.
Dependency or program drift|Pinned versions, advisory review, binary/source comparison, licence inventory and revalidation after upgrades.
Denial of wallet or service|Rate limits, bounded jobs, retry budgets, cost alarms, circuit breakers and degraded read-only mode.
ENDTABLE
H2|14.3 Incident and recovery procedure
Provide independent switches for serving, new participation, launch preparation, launch funding, settlement publication and application claim preparation. The scope must be honest: disabling website claims cannot stop direct on-chain claims unless the selected program supports that authority. Do not assume pausing a site protects already-compromised custody.
On a suspicious event, stop new financial preparation/funding, preserve logs and signatures, classify affected intents, reconcile chain state and publish a clear status update. Never blindly resend after a timeout. Recover workers from checkpoints under a fresh fenced lease. Resume only after the cause, financial exposure and corrective action are documented. Keep withdrawals/legitimate claims available wherever safe and technically independent of the failing component.
Proposed operating targets are detection within 15 minutes during a supervised pilot, daily balance reconciliation, and recovery of read-only service within four hours of a routine failure. A solo founder cannot promise continuous 24-hour human coverage. Outside staffed windows, automation should stop risky new work and retain durable evidence rather than continue unattended financial launch operations.
H2|14.4 Privacy and legal release review
Collect only identity links and evidence needed for eligibility, fraud review, consent and accounting. Never collect wallet secrets. Define deletion/retention for content copies, IP-derived abuse signals and notification tokens; distinguish removable private data from public on-chain history and immutable financial records. Proposed raw security-log retention is 30 days, with longer financial evidence retention determined by legal/accounting requirements.
Before public financial release, obtain Australian legal advice on the actual rights and activities: token distribution, NFT-linked allocations, custody, staking incentives, marketing, consumer obligations, tax/accounting and any applicable licensing or AML requirements. ASIC's public guidance emphasizes substance over labels; calling a product an NFT or community reward does not establish its classification. Paid randomized upgrades/rerolls and social promotions need their own review. This PRD does not conclude that any feature is exempt or legally approved. [S12]
H1|15 Operating cost model and budgets
H2|15.1 Cost discipline
All figures below are planning inputs, not current transaction quotations or spending approvals. Keep SOL quantities separate from USD conversion. Let P be USD per SOL, F be transaction plus priority fees, R be account rent, Q be non-refundable protocol fees and H be hosting/provider expense. Cash needed is P × (F + R + Q) + H. Rent can be recoverable only where the program permits account closure and liabilities have ended; permanently locked or required accounts still consume cash.
Record payer separately for every line: founder, minter, trader, staker or claimant. A zero purchase price is not a zero-cost mint. A claimant-funded ATA removes founder cash outlay but still adds user friction. Do not subsidize repeated claims or thousands of mints without a bounded campaign budget. Never use unclaimed user rewards as working capital.
Historical repository evidence from October 30 recorded 0.000005 SOL base fee, 0.0010668 SOL rent for an 82-byte mint, and 0.00148844 SOL for a 165-byte token account. These are dated reference inputs; extensions, account size, signatures, priority fees and program requirements change actual costs. The earlier 0.00626792 SOL local custody estimate used synthetic assumptions and is not a complete mainnet launch price. [R7]

H1|16 Economic implications and treasury
H2|16.1 Genesis weight and accrued value
At illustrative supply S = 1 billion and a 2.5% Genesis budget, Level 1 receives approximately 1241.927 ATT and Level 10 approximately 12419.275 ATT per launch before base-unit rounding. A complete tranche represents exactly one tenth of Genesis weight, so it receives 2.5 million ATT from that 25 million budget regardless of when it mints. If only one complete tranche is minted, 90% of the Genesis budget remains reserved for unminted tranches. When no Season is active, these amounts double.
At a nominal $10000 FDV, the total 2.5% Genesis allocation has a $250 mark value, and a Level 1 share is about $0.0124 per launch. These are paper values, not realizable proceeds. Thirty identical launches would imply about $0.37 at those marks for Level 1, before market changes or execution costs. This illustrates why selling a mint on guaranteed yield or treating nominal allocations as cash would be misleading.
For an NFT with historical unclaimed amounts a_j, indicative Vault value = sum of a_j × current token price_j. A buyer's net outcome also depends on mint price, fees, remaining vesting, actual liquidity and future prices. Unminted accrual can create later-mint demand but also concentrated arbitrage and price pressure; fair allocation and disclosed phase prices are essential.
H2|16.2 Season weights and progression
With no upgrades, one Season's active weight by completed month is 2013, 1524, 1012, 628, 372, 212, 116, 60, 28, 10 and then zero. The values follow the expiry of each Level cohort. Ten monthly overlapping cohorts would total 5975 active weight immediately after a new Season begins, assuming aligned month boundaries, full reserved inventory and no progression. Actual calendar dates and upgrades change the live denominator.
The original Level distribution has expected Level 2.013. Upgrading two Level L NFTs changes their combined expected Level from 2L to L + 1 + 2.013, an expected change of 3.013 − L. At Level 1 this is +2.013; at Level 9 it is −5.987. The target improves, but the material can lose substantial Level and future benefit time. Present that tradeoff before payment; do not describe every upgrade as increasing total economic weight.
At a 0.1 SOL working mint price, 1000 paid mints produce 100 SOL gross purchase revenue, while free/founder mints produce none. This is a scenario, not a demand forecast. Subtract mint sponsorship, protocol/transaction charges, artwork, support, tax and other costs. For repeated visual rerolls, the sum of the first n fees is 0.01 × (2^n − 1) SOL; costs rise rapidly and must never be hidden behind a generic confirmation.
H2|16.3 Earn and fee sustainability
At illustrative S = 1 billion, a daily Earn budget is 10 million ATT; the per-wallet daily maximum is 1 million ATT. At a $10000 nominal FDV this has a $10 mark per token, with a possible ten-token basket if all campaigns have those marks. It is neither a guaranteed payout value nor liquid income. Small winner sets increase per-user allocations but also cause burns; the cap makes fewer than ten winners structurally unable to exhaust a daily budget.
At the documented default 20% protocol split and 1% ongoing trading fee, simplified company fee revenue = verified trading notional × 0.008, assuming all remaining LP fees are claimable by the company and no other deductions. For $100, $1000 and $10000 of volume, that gives $0.80, $8 and $80 before costs. Use actual fee receipts rather than aggregating unreliable volume feeds. The high opening fee must not be the core sustainability assumption.
If all-in variable cost is K dollars per launch and allocated monthly fixed cost is H/30, simplified break-even daily volume is (K + H/30) / 0.008. For an illustrative K = $1 and H = $5, it is about $145.83. These are scenario inputs, not measured launch costs. With zero trading volume and no NFT revenue, thirty launches cost 30K + H; under the same example that is $35, before other liabilities and labor. A few losing days must trigger review before a daily cadence consumes the remaining runway.
H2|16.4 Treasury policy
Company revenue can include realized ATT fees, paid Genesis/Season mints, received royalties, upgrades, rerolls and AI refresh margin. Reconcile gross receipts, protocol deductions, sponsored costs and net operating contribution separately. Do not count user principal, staking reserves, unclaimed Earn/Vault allocations, founder-marked inventory or unrealized token prices as available operating revenue.
The handoff proposes converting 50% of realized company token revenue to USDC and retaining 50%, subject to runway needs. Keep this discretionary and subordinate to essential operating cash. Conversion has fees, slippage, market risk and authorization requirements. Treasury automation is deferred; initial conversions require a human-approved quote and signed transaction. A 30000-user Farcaster airdrop is an optional separate experiment, not approved launch scope; at a $10000 nominal FDV a 10% supply airdrop would mark only about $0.033 per recipient before costs.

H2|17.2 Release sequence
Phase A is feasibility and specification validation. Finish policy questions, isolate reusable code, validate the package matrix, build deterministic economic fixtures and execute protocol/cost spikes locally. Deliver recorded evidence for G1–G5, not a new live product. Require no paid service by default. A failure that changes confirmed economics returns to the founder with a concrete alternative.
Phase B is a controlled market pilot. A nonfinancial preview can test discovery and design immediately after implementation approval. A mainnet production ATT pilot requires all promised allocations and custody paths to be safe, including the unminted Genesis reserve and any applicable Earn/Stake obligations. Launch at most one approved campaign initially, with an operator present and a complete stop/recovery plan. A separate limited protocol experiment must not be marketed as a fully featured ATT campaign.
Phase C exposes complete Vault → Stake → Earn flows. These mechanics must already protect funds at the first production campaign even if dashboards are introduced gradually. Validate claims, moderation, token-day evidence and global settlement across up to ten concurrent campaigns. Proposed scale-up condition is three consecutive campaign settlements reconciled with zero unexplained balance variance, no unresolved critical security issue, and observed cost within the approved budget.
Phase D opens Genesis tranches. The reserve starts with production ATT allocations, while public minting waits for fair assignment, metadata and marketplace validation. Begin with the first curated tranche. Review paid conversion, concentration, support cost and real revenue before choosing among ASAP, 7, 14 and 30 days for the next opening. Founder inventory cannot substitute for demand evidence.
Phase E introduces monthly Seasons after expiry snapshots, free eligibility, upgrades, rerolls and historical claims pass their additional tests and legal review. Launching Seasons later does not retroactively divert old Genesis fallback allocations. Delay optional premium mechanics until paid behavior supports their complexity.
Phase F adds justified growth features: richer marketplace execution, optional host distribution, agent integrations, AI-assisted moderation and eventually other chains. Each needs measured demand, a cost owner and compatibility tests. There is no calendar promise in this roadmap; progression is evidence- and cash-gated.

Make onboarding actions configurable and reset content streaks after 24 hours without a qualifying approval. Sections 4–7 define the behavior; section 18 gives the checks.
The founder permits either historical mint-release policy and delegated the purchase-credit design. The selected defaults preserve original Vault vesting and count verified purchases followed by continued staking. A morning or evening Sydney window can be chosen later without changing token economics.
H2|22.2 Two checks before real money
Who can claim after a sale. Suppose a Genesis NFT has 100 vested ATT. Alice claims 30 and sells it to Bob. Bob must be able to claim the remaining 70; Alice must no longer be able to claim anything. A website balance is a display. The program holding the funds must verify the current owner and prevent a second payment. Test an existing program first. If none meets those requirements within budget, bring back the failed test, a costed alternative and what would need to wait. No custom program or spending decision is assumed.
What stakers receive. Five percent is the amount 10X sets aside. The unresolved fact is whether a candidate farm deducts a fee when it pays users. Section 6 shows the numbers. Verify an actual configuration and a claim before presenting a choice. If it takes no fee and otherwise passes, there is no fee decision to make.
H2|22.3 Genesis voting and sales
Confirmed electorate. Current Solana Genesis holder wallets and wallets on the next tranche's whitelist can vote on the timing of that tranche.
The four-way advisory vote runs for 48 hours with one vote per eligible wallet and results published at any turnout. Each Genesis sale stage has 48 hours of public notice. Section 8 gives the confirmed timing, price and per-stage mint limits.
H2|22.4 Operating policy and remaining choices
Set later Genesis stage prices and publish the timetable before a sale. Only revealed Genesis 1X holdings qualify for the free Season mint. Earn awards remain claimable indefinitely, and published scoring and eligibility decisions are final. 10X permanently omits NFT burning as a product action.
Aim below US$10 for the first paid test, with a founder-approved exception up to US$30 when necessary. Treat the 50% cash reserve as a planning guideline. The combined ceiling for new recurring services is US$5 per month. The founder can review these limits later. Before spending, present a quote and verified cash on hand. Select wallets, marketplaces and randomness providers after the mobile signing, fair assignment and cost tests. Choose the staffed Sydney launch window before publishing it.
